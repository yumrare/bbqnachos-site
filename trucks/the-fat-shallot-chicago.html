Chicago, Illinois · Featured Truck

The Fat Shallot

Cuisine: Sandwiches
Signature: Truffle BLT and salami sandwiches
Home base: Chicago, Illinois
Rolling since 2013

The Fat Shallot never chased a trend; it chased its regulars, and caught them. The Sandwiches kitchen out of Chicago, Illinois runs on repeat orders and first-name greetings at the window.

The Fat Shallot has been rolling since 2013. Chef Sam Barron's Chicago truck, a Loop lunchtime fixture.

A truck is carried by one dish more than any other, and The Fat Shallot is carried by its truffle blt and salami sandwiches. First-timers order it on a tip; everybody else orders it from pure memory.

Chicago counts its trucks among its landmarks, and The Fat Shallot is on that list: the dependable stop regulars bring out-of-towners to first, because this is the one that explains the city.

There is no freezer-to-fryer shrug at The Fat Shallot. The Sandwiches cooking is prepped each morning, seasoned like somebody's reputation depends on it — because at a truck window, it does.

Start with a handful of names if you want to understand Chicago's truck scene; The Fat Shallot is on the first page. Its influence shows in the trucks that followed and the routes eaters plan around its window.

The afterglow separates The Fat Shallot from a hundred good trucks: people leave planning the next visit and recruiting companions for it. That is not marketing — that is a signature landing.

What makes The Fat Shallot itself, and no other truck in this record, is the combination it runs on: Sandwiches out of Chicago, with truffle blt and salami sandwiches as the order its name is attached to.

Plenty of trucks cook well; fewer become reference points. The Fat Shallot is one for Chicago — the name the city reaches for when it explains its truck scene to a stranger.

Popular is one thing; foundational is another. A generation of Chicago eaters learned what a food truck could be at the The Fat Shallot window, and the lesson stuck.

The Fat Shallot stands in the permanent record with its home base, cuisine, and signature stated plainly. Holding a line here means still being the truck your city says you are when the quarterly review comes around.
