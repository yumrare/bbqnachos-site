The Halal Guys

Cuisine: Middle Eastern
Signature: Chicken and gyro over rice with white sauce
Home base: New York, New York
Rolling since 1990

In New York, New York, the name The Halal Guys means Middle Eastern done the food truck way — cooked to order, handed through a window, and eaten wherever the day finds you. The Halal Guys holds a place in this directory's record of the nation's most popular trucks, a standing earned the slow way, one service at a time.

The Halal Guys has been rolling since 1990. Began as a Midtown hot dog cart in 1990 before the halal platters made the corner of 53rd and Sixth famous.

If The Halal Guys could only serve one thing, the record would still read the same: chicken and gyro over rice with white sauce. It is the dish the truck built its name on, the one photographed, recommended, and argued about in the best way.

Serious eaters make for serious competition in New York, and The Halal Guys has held its place in that company for good reason. The city's whole truck culture runs on trucks exactly like this one.

At The Halal Guys the Middle Eastern is cooked to order and handed out hot, built for curbs, tailgates, and park benches. Speed is part of the recipe, and fast is never confused with careless.

Some trucks are popular; The Halal Guys is foundational. In New York, its window is where a generation of eaters learned what a food truck could be, and the lesson stuck.

The regulars say the real signature is the rhythm — truck posted up, line forming, window sliding open. The Halal Guys has it down to a ritual, and rituals turn good trucks into beloved ones.

The Halal Guys's originality is a pairing, not a gimmick: New York as the stage, Middle Eastern as the craft, and chicken and gyro over rice with white sauce as the headline act.

That is the entry for The Halal Guys: New York's own, a Middle Eastern truck with a signature worth the trip. It takes its place in the national record alongside the rest of the thousand, each one somebody's favorite truck in America.
