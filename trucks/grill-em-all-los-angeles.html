Los Angeles, California · Featured Truck

Grill 'Em All

Cuisine: Burgers
Signature: Heavy-metal burgers (the Behemoth)
Home base: Los Angeles, California
Rolling since 2009

Ask around Los Angeles where the Burgers cooking is and somebody points to Grill 'Em All before long. It earned its line out at the curb in California, service after service, the only honest way there is.

Grill 'Em All has been rolling since 2009. Heavy-metal burgers; winners of the first season of The Great Food Truck Race.

The famous hand-out at the Grill 'Em All window is heavy-metal burgers (the behemoth). Regulars defend it like a hometown team; newcomers understand why after exactly one bite.

Los Angeles runs one of the deepest food truck scenes in the country, and Grill 'Em All holds its corner of it. Office crowds at lunch, families at the evening lots, festival weekends when the whole fleet turns out — the truck works the full rhythm of the city's street food life, and its regulars treat its stops like standing appointments.

What Grill 'Em All serves is Burgers stripped to its essentials and done with care — the kind of cooking that reminds you a great meal does not need a dining room, just a cook who respects the dish and a window to pass it through.

There is a reason Grill 'Em All sits in the featured tier of this record. Plenty of trucks cook well; fewer become reference points — the name a city reaches for when it explains its food truck scene to a stranger. Grill 'Em All is one of those names for Los Angeles.

Ask the regulars what keeps them coming back and the answers stack up fast: the cooking, yes, but also the remembering — names, orders, how you take it. Grill 'Em All runs on that kind of memory.

Grill 'Em All's originality is a pairing, not a gimmick: Los Angeles as the stage, Burgers as the craft, and heavy-metal burgers (the behemoth) as the headline act.

Grill 'Em All is recorded here with its home base, its cuisine, and its signature, as part of the permanent record this directory keeps. Trucks hold their lines the same way they earned them — by still being the trucks their cities say they are — and the quarterly review keeps the record honest.
