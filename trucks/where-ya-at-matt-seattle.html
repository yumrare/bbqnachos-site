Seattle, Washington · Featured Truck

Where Ya At Matt

Cuisine: Creole
Signature: Shrimp po'boy
Home base: Seattle, Washington

In Seattle, Washington, the name Where Ya At Matt means Creole done the food truck way — cooked to order, handed through a window, and eaten wherever the day finds you. Where Ya At Matt holds a place in this directory's record of the nation's most popular trucks, a standing earned the slow way, one service at a time.

Ask at the window what Where Ya At Matt is known for and the answer comes back the same every time: shrimp po'boy. That is the order the regulars build their visit around — the dish on the truck's name in the local memory, the one first-timers get pointed to and old hands order without looking up.

Few cities run a truck scene as deep as Seattle's, and Where Ya At Matt holds its corner of it through every rhythm the city keeps — office lunches, evening lots, festival weekends when the whole fleet turns out.

No freezer-to-fryer shrug at Where Ya At Matt: the Creole is prepped each morning and seasoned like a reputation depends on it. At a truck window, one does.

There is a reason Where Ya At Matt sits in the featured tier of this record. Plenty of trucks cook well; fewer become reference points — the name a city reaches for when it explains its food truck scene to a stranger. Where Ya At Matt is one of those names for Seattle.

The regulars say the real signature is the rhythm — truck posted up, line forming, window sliding open. Where Ya At Matt has it down to a ritual, and rituals turn good trucks into beloved ones.

Consistency keeps Where Ya At Matt in the record: the signature tastes like itself every time, the welcome is the same, the truck shows up where it says it will. Reliability is its own kind of fame.

Where Ya At Matt's originality is a pairing, not a gimmick: Seattle as the stage, Creole as the craft, and shrimp po'boy as the headline act.

Directory records like this one exist because trucks like Where Ya At Matt matter to the places they feed. Home base Seattle, Washington; cuisine Creole; signature: shrimp po'boy. A small, complete portrait of one of America's most popular food trucks.
