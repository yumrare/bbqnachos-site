San Francisco, California · Featured Truck

The Chairman

Cuisine: Taiwanese bao
Signature: Pork belly bao
Home base: San Francisco, California

Ask around San Francisco where the Taiwanese bao cooking is and somebody points to The Chairman before long. It earned its line out at the curb in California, service after service, the only honest way there is.

pork belly bao: that is the signature at The Chairman, and it makes the whole argument for the truck in a single order. This is the dish San Francisco crosses town for.

Show up, season after season, with the same quality and the same welcome: that is what the San Francisco scene rewards, and it is the reputation The Chairman carries — a stop locals give directions by.

At The Chairman, Taiwanese bao is a craft of constraints: a small grill, a short ticket, nowhere to hide. What comes through the window argues that constraints, respected, make cooking better.

To understand San Francisco's truck scene, you start with a handful of names, and The Chairman is on that first page. Its influence shows up in the trucks that came after it and in the eaters who plan their routes around its window.

There is a small ceremony to a stop at The Chairman: the chalkboard read, the order called, the wait spent trading notes with strangers who are about to agree with you. Trucks that create rituals outlive trucks that only create meals.

The regulars say the real signature is the rhythm — truck posted up, line forming, window sliding open. The Chairman has it down to a ritual, and rituals turn good trucks into beloved ones.

The honest entry is the distinct one, and The Chairman's writes itself: Taiwanese bao from San Francisco, California, with pork belly bao as the dish the city attached to its name.

Popular is one thing; foundational is another. A generation of San Francisco eaters learned what a food truck could be at the The Chairman window, and the lesson stuck.

The quarterly review will come back to The Chairman the way it comes back to every truck in this book — checking that the window is still open, the line still forms, and the signature still sings. Nobody around here expects a different result.
